GPU & HBM liquid cold plate

I’m designing an additively manufactured copper cold plate for a 1.49 kW GPU/HBM thermal test vehicle: two GPU dies with hot cores up to 200 W/cm², surrounded by eight HBM memory stacks, all cooled by 45 °C water at 2.25 L/min. Designs are scored on how far they lower peak GPU and HBM temperatures compared with a baseline fin plate, with HBM weighted at 70% and a penalty for pressure drop above 4 psi.

I’m handling the full design and analysis myself, from the conjugate heat transfer model in Ansys Fluent and the hand calculations that check it, to screening fin geometries and developing a fin concept around the limits of fine-feature copper printing.

Approach & current progress

Heat-load model: I wrote a script that converts the competition’s spreadsheet power map into a Fluent heat-flux UDF covering all 630 heater cells. It checks that the cell powers add up to the published totals, and in tests on grids that don’t line up with the heater cells, the integrated load stayed within 0.5% of 1,492.8 W.

Hand calculations: An energy balance puts the mixed outlet near 54.6 °C, and a laminar channel model gives a Reynolds number around 110 and roughly 0.5 psi of friction loss through the covered GPU channels. These set expectations for the CFD and serve as convergence sanity checks.

Fin screening: Before committing to CFD, I compared 169 straight-fin thickness and spacing combinations with a reduced-order fin model to bound the design space against the printer’s minimum feature size.

Meshing and run time: Resolving every 0.1 mm fin channel takes several cells across each gap, which puts a full-plate mesh at an estimated 15 to 20 million cells. The manifold, fins and power map are mirror-symmetric about the plane through the outlet slot, so after confirming that symmetry on the extracted fluid body, I model half the plate with a symmetry boundary. That halves the cell count and the compute per run, which matters when every design candidate needs its own converged solution.

Consistent comparisons: The baseline and each candidate run with identical materials, temperature-dependent water and silicon properties, boundary conditions and reporting. Results need a mesh-independence study and a closed heat balance before they count.

Next: Mesh-independence checks, candidate comparisons scored with the competition’s figure of merit, and the white paper. I’ll post design details and final results here after submission.
