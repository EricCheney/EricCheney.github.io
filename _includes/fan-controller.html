Cost optimization for manufacturing

Before ordering, I reworked the board and bill of materials against the manufacturer’s pricing, cutting roughly $150 from the two-prototype order, about a third of the original cost.

  • Bare board: $88 → $33 (−62%). Switched the finish from ENIG to OSP ($88 → $70), then enlarged all 209 of the 0.20 mm vias to 0.30 mm, which moved the six-layer board into the fab’s standard via tier and halved the price again. Fitting the larger vias meant rerouting a dense area under the USB-C PD controller and re-verifying clearances against the fab’s limits, while keeping the epoxy-filled, copper-capped vias the via-in-pad layout needs.
  • Inductors: about $23 saved per board. Replaced two premium inductors with parts sized to each converter’s actual current limit ($15.07 → $0.06 and $11.40 → $3.78), confirming saturation current against the regulators’ peak limits.
  • Assembly fees: $30 saved per order. Moved 20 passive lines to the assembler’s basic parts library, which has no per-line loading fee, and merged near-duplicate values.
  • Sourcing risk removed. Replaced parts that were out of stock at the assembler with in-stock equivalents of the same specification (for example, bulk capacitors at $0.63 instead of $1.75), avoiding global-sourcing fees and delays.
Architecture, design decisions & current progress

Power and control: A USB-C Power Delivery input feeds a programmable buck-boost converter. A dedicated STM32 handles power supervision and sensing, while an ESP32-S3 display module provides the planned interface. The design includes an output eFuse, voltage and current monitoring, temperature sensing, and watchdog-controlled enables.

Design decisions: The three fan outputs share a voltage rail, with independent tachometer inputs. Current sensing uses dedicated Kelvin connections. An 80 × 80 mm removable frame provides tooling holes and fiducials for assembly of the 60 mm circular board. Via-in-pad lands require epoxy-filled, copper-capped vias throughout.

Current progress: Two assembled prototypes were ordered from JLCPCB in September 2026. Before ordering, the layout passed electrical-rule, design-rule and schematic-parity checks, part orientations were verified against the assembler’s placement preview, and the bill of materials and fabrication options were optimized for cost. Firmware, the printed enclosure, and electrical and thermal bench validation are next. The 18 V ceiling is an experimental setting above a 12 V fan’s rating; the 2 A combined continuous-output target is not yet bench-validated.
