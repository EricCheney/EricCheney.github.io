Course team project / ME 329 / Spring 2026

Hand-winch gearbox

My contribution: Engineering analysis of a gearbox adapted from a Harbor Freight hand winch.

Team result: 275 lb tested maximum load from a $56 build. Our final design review reported the strongest performance per dollar in the class.

Cost, load testing & engineering analysis

Starting point: An existing 40:1 worm-drive hand winch. The team modified the spool, output shaft, and handle; the gearing was adapted from the commercial unit.

Report analysis: Shaft bending and torsion, keyway and shoulder stress concentrations, fatigue, gear behavior, and fastening loads.

Engineering lesson: The keyway was an analytical concern, but the shaft-to-spool connection limited the physical test.

Limitations: The report’s shaft-fatigue result fell below its stated target. Short-duration load testing did not validate fatigue life. A stronger shaft connection and material changes were proposed for future iterations.

Course team project / ME 328 / Winter 2026

PVC knee scooter

My contribution: CAD and finite element analysis for the team’s Scooterrari knee scooter.

Design work: Developed the scooter CAD and evaluated frame bending and column buckling using finite element analysis.

CAD, structural analysis & design decisions

The team targeted a 160 lb rider load and less than 0.25 in deflection. The report compares hand calculations with a beam-element FEA model of the horizontal frame members.

Analysis approach: Compared hand calculations with beam-element FEA for the horizontal frame and used buckling studies to assess the steering column and seat post under modeled loads.

Team recommendations: Increase frame stiffness, shorten the horizontal members, and improve steering and wheel support. These were proposed improvements, not validated revisions.
